sources concern finite covers, one-sided bounds and obstacles to some methods.

Scope and remaining inputs

  For the proposed O(x² / (log x)^(401/200)) upper bound,
  log_saving_of_two_sieves requires a lower bound on the number of survivors
  and a uniform upper bound per progression. The TeX note gives the intended
  sources and normalizations. Both sieve inequalities still require proofs.
  The former harmonic hypothesis follows from Harmonique687.band_bound:
  for 2 <= z <= x, log(z) times the sum of 1/p over z < p <= x is at most
  log(x) - log(z) + 10. The proof uses the factorial convolution for the
  von Mangoldt function and Chebyshev's upper bound for its partial sums.

  For a qualitative saving, GainVariable687.exists_log_saving only needs
  some positive stock constant c and progression constant K, uniform over
  the cutoff exponents 0 < d <= 1/2. Each threshold in x may depend on d.
  Its proof chooses d = min(1/2, c/(6*K)), bounds the reciprocal-prime sum
  by 3*d*log(log x)/log x, and obtains exponent 2+2*d. The later theorem
  gain_from_stock supplies its progression hypothesis. The stronger fixed
  exponent 401/200 still has the numerical hypotheses of log_saving_of_two_sieves.

  DemiBande687.H2_proved discharges H2 of the research R2 reduction
  unconditionally. For x>=1024 and x/2<p<=x, J=floor(x^2/p)+1 satisfies
  x<=J<=2x and its integer eighth root is <=floor(x/2). The upstream
  bound of 17 J/log J therefore applies directly. If d=floor(x/(log2(x)+1)),
  where log2 is the integer base-two logarithm, the verified real comparison
  x/log x <= (15/8)d gives at most 64d deletions per prime. Chebyshev gives
  at most 15d primes, so the total is <=960 d^2, below the 1000 d^2 budget.
  R2 is in this research fork. The public catalog's R1.H2 is the separate
  Mertens statement.

  Blocs687.H1_of_interface proves R2.H1 from the same quantitative survivor input. At
  z=floor(x/2), partition [1,x^2] into full blocks of length N=height(z)
  and a remainder. Translate the residues independently in each full block.
  Each contributes at least c' N log log z/(log z)^2, and the full blocks
  contain at least x^2/2 points for large x since N=o(x^2). Consequently
  the total exceeds 1000 floor(x/(log2(x)+1))^2 eventually. The block
  summation is the reusable lemma Blocs687.blocks.

  An earlier paper-only route to the same dyadic H2 used five blocks:
  put z=floor(x/2). For p>z the index interval
  of length floor(x^2/p)+1 fits in five blocks of length z for large x.
  Translate each block's residues, extend the last block to length z, and
  apply the proved 255*(1+z/log z) sieve bound to each. All primes in
  (ceil(x/2),x] divide binom(x,floor(x/2)), whose size is at most 2^x;
  at most one endpoint prime was omitted. This gives asymptotic upper
  coefficient (5*255/2)*log 2 for total deletions in units x^2/(log x)^2.
  R2's threshold has coefficient 1000*(log 2)^2, strictly larger because
  log 2 > 0.6375. This avoids needing the smaller per-prime constant 32.

Quantitative survivor theorem

  Theorem 1.2 of the OpenAI preprint uses exactly the height, width and scale
  in InterfaceSource687.statement. In StockSource687, shifting i to i+1
  translates the authors' interval to the positive interval used for Y.
  Write L=log z and w=L/(log L)^2. The accepted Mertens product proof gives
  product(floor w) >= 1/(3 log w), and eventually log w >= (log L)/2.
  Thus the coefficient c in Theorem 1.2 gives c/6 times N log L/L^2
  at the real cutoff z, and c/12 in GainVariable687.stockBound at
  z=x/(log x)^d for 0<d<=1/2. The absolute progression constant is 35.
  Hence one may take eta=2 min(1/2,c/2520) on paper. The coefficient c
  is the positive constant of Theorem 1.2, not a numerical estimate.

  JacobsthalExtension687.gain concerns the general Jacobsthal
  function h(k) for integers with at most k distinct prime divisors.
  Take x=ceil(2 k log k), z=x/(log x)^d and N=height(z). Prescribe extra
  arbitrary residues for all primes up to z not dividing the given integer.
  Taking d small enough leaves an absolute positive fraction of the lower
  stock after all primes in (z,x] are removed, even including nondivisors.
  For each remaining divisor prime p>x, its progression of indices fits
  in [0,floor(N/x)+1). Every q<=z still forbids one class of indices,
  since p is invertible modulo q. Enlarge to that whole index interval;
  its length is <=z, is asymptotic to N/x and has logarithm asymptotic to
  log x. The uniform upper sieve bounds the loss by O(N/(x log x)) per
  divisor. The k such losses total O(N/(log x)^2), negligible beside the
  remaining stock of order N log log x/(log x)^2. This gives
  h(k)=O(k^2/(log k)^(2d)) on the same published input. The checked
  transfer uses the catalog definition with arbitrary integer starting
  points and at most k distinct prime divisors. In SurvieTotale687.robust_stock
  the count after the full sieve and the extra primes still exceeds or equals
  (c/4) N log log x/(log x)^2, where c is the normalized cutoff stock constant.

Extending an optimal cover

  Translate the old interval by J. Assign different fresh primes to each
  point in the uncovered prefix of J positions. Extension687 proves this
  finite construction and applies it to an optimal cover. When z >= k,
  the existence of k survivors for some choice of old classes is exactly
  the existence of an admissible k-element subset of [0,J). For p>k,
  counting residues alone guarantees an omitted class. Consequently, if
  there are r fresh primes, z>=r+1, and H(r+1) is the smallest diameter
  of an admissible (r+1)-tuple, then Y(x)>=Y(z)+H(r+1). The interval
  [0,H(r+1)) has diameter strictly less than H(r+1).

  For example the prime number theorem gives, on paper, the unconditional
  increment Y(x)>=Y(floor(x/2))+floor(x/40) for all sufficiently large x.
  With J=floor(x/40) the verified 17 J/log J sieve applies since
  256<=J<=floor(x/2). Its upper coefficient 17/40 is strictly below
  the asymptotic coefficient 1/2 for the number of primes in (x/2,x].
  Apply growth_from_capacity. The invocation of the prime number theorem
  in this dyadic asymptotic corollary is outside the local Lean audit.

  For r=7 a separate direct calculation needs only z>=7 and gives a gain
  of 26. At length 27 the points 0,2,6,8,12,18,20,26 form an admissible
  eight-tuple. This shows the limit of the uniform single-prime-per-point
  test; it does not bound how far a particular optimal cover can extend.
  More generally, any k primes in (k,m] give an admissible k-tuple in
  [0,m+1), as certified by prime_tuple_barrier. By the prime number theorem,
  m=(1+epsilon) k log k suffices for any fixed positive epsilon and large k.
  Thus taking r of order x/log x yields at most an order-x increment from
  the uniform singleton test alone. Beyond this scale one needs extra
  information about the old cover or a fresh class capturing several points.
  Paires687.covered_iff_pairs proves the exact condition used by the residual
  pair search in recherche_687.py when no residue class hits more than two
  remaining points. With point set S and prime set P, one needs at least
  |S|-|P| disjoint congruent pairs, using a different prime for each pair.
  The sufficient direction holds for classes of arbitrary size.

  The quotient indices of any single fresh prime residue class again avoid
  a prescribed residue for each old prime. CapaciteParite687.class_indices
  proves this for J <= p*k, with quotient indices in [0,k). The wheel for
  primes 2 and 3 bounds the number of such indices among six by two. Thus
  J <= 6(z+1) suffices for the disjoint-pair criterion when z>=3. At
  J <= 2(z+1), z>=2, each fresh prime covers at most one survivor, and
  coverage is equivalent to the cardinality bound. For z>=13, the length-27
  eight-point witness therefore defeats every seven-prime assignment for
  some choice of old residues. This concerns the uniform prefix test.
  The two sides of a given maximal old cover can have different profiles.
  Conversely each admissible m-tuple in the quotient interval can already
  be realized in one class of a fresh prime when z>=m. Theorem class_iff_tuple
  gives the exact existential equivalence. An improvement to the worst-case
  load of a single class must therefore also bound admissible prime tuples.

  The endpoint refinement uses an additional fresh prime dividing J.
  Suppose every length-J prefix has at most r survivors, uniformly over
  residues. Among the survivors in a prefix of length J+1, erasing either
  endpoint leaves at most r points (translate the interval for the left
  endpoint). If more than r survive, both endpoints must be present and
  there are exactly r+1 points. A fresh prime dividing J takes both; the
  other r-1 primes suffice. This gives Y(x)>=Y(z)+J+1. For z>=2 a maximal
  cover has odd length: an even-length cover could be extended on one side
  by its existing class of 2. For J=26 and 13 among seven fresh primes,
  the resulting increment is at least 28. Source: Extremites687.lean.
  Independently enumerating the 210 residue choices at 2,3,5,7 on [0,27)
  gives respectively 4,32,102,68,4 assignments with 4,5,6,7,8 survivors.
  In all four eight-survivor cases, 0 and 26 occur together.

  For an actual old cover, its left and right 27-position extensions use
  translated versions of the same four residues. Bords687.growth_outside_nine
  proves Y(x)>=Y(z)+28 whenever z>=7, there are seven or more new primes,
  and Y(z) modulo 210 lies outside {21,27,33,123,129,135,177,183,189}.
  In particular every old maximal length indivisible by 3 qualifies.
  The kernel checks the complete four-profile list and its translations.
  This uses information about the old cover that the uniform 26 test discards.

  bords_687.py checks all 29 splits of 28 positions between the two ends.
  Maximality forces both old boundary points to survive. For the 2304 eligible
  small-prime profiles, the best split leaves 4,5,6,7,8 survivors in respectively
  51,596,1173,473,11 cases. The eleven cases have lengths modulo 210 in
  {21,27,33,123,177,183}. In each, the two one-sided extensions have eight
  survivors apiece. An old prime must avoid all sixteen positions to preserve
  the obstruction. Incorporating 11 and 13 leaves 683 exceptional odd lengths
  modulo 30030 out of 15015. Each prime >=17 can individually miss the union
  of the sixteen positions. These enumerations concern the boundary model;
  realization as a maximal old cover is an additional condition.

  Recouvrements687.lean gives a different finite upper test. On the old
  survivor set S let t(n) be the number of fresh classes hitting n and put
  M_j=sum_{n in S} binom(t(n),j). If M_1+M_3<|S|+M_2 for every assignment,
  then at least one point avoids all classes and Y(x)<=N. For coprime p,q
  and N<pq their prescribed pair of classes meets [1,N] at most once.
  At a power cutoff z=x^(1-delta), the harmonic mass of (z,x] approaches a
  positive constant. The present first-order upper bound for losses is then
  O(N/log x), whereas the guaranteed stock is of order N log log x/log^2 x.
  Thus the current comparison cannot give a fixed power saving in x.
  Controlling the excess M_2-M_3 from below would be one way forward;
  the pairwise upper bound alone does not provide this.

  Repeating a first-order deletion bound over adjacent bands also has a
  limitation. For cutoffs x/(log x)^d with fixed d, every increment in d
  incurs a loss in the guaranteed coefficient of N log log x/(log x)^2.
  Adding the bounds for contiguous bands adds those losses. Dividing the
  same band into more pieces cannot replenish its original survivor count.
  Any iteration seeking a power saving in x needs additional information
  on the surviving points or on intersections of the deleted classes.

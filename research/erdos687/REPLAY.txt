Erdős 687(i) | Research handoff from francoisbrunel with Codex

The open target asks for the order of Y up to constant factors. The companion
sources concern finite covers, one-sided bounds and obstacles to some methods.

Proof sources

  Couvertures687.lean        Finite witnesses for 47, 71 and 73, and the
                            explicit double cover of the 22 admissible points.
  Exclusion687.lean          Soundness of exhaustive rejection using a sum of
                            prime capacities, with two bounded applications.
  Comptage687.lean           Explicit bound on pi(x) in the integer
                            normalization used by the progression estimate.
  ReductionIwaniec.lean      Replay name of the R2 reduction source

                            Erdos687.Y, including Y(37) = 65, Y(47) >= 99,

  Progressions687.lean       Uses the Chinese remainder theorem to embed the
                            survivors of one progression in a sifted interval.
  Protection687.lean         For any protected set and any band of moduli,
                            proves additive and multiplicative guarantees
                            for simultaneously avoiding nonzero residues.
  Harmonique687.lean         Finite von Mangoldt estimate and upper bound on
                            the reciprocal primes in the exact cutoff band.
  DeuxEstimations687.lean    Specializes the transfer to two sieve hypotheses.
  GainVariable687.lean       Chooses the cutoff after the stock and progression
                            constants are known. Proves the conditional result
                            with some exponent strictly larger than 2.
  CribleOAI687.lean          Generated from four OpenAI files by
                            rejouer_crible_687.py. This is the upstream Selberg
                            upper sieve for intervals with arbitrary residues.
  CribleTransfert687.lean    Reconciles the index conventions, gives the
                            uniform progression constant 1275, and reduces
                            the qualitative log saving to the survivor count.

Scope and remaining inputs

  For the proposed O(x² / (log x)^(401/200)) upper bound,
  log_saving_of_two_sieves requires a lower bound on the number of survivors
  and a uniform upper bound per progression. The TeX note gives the intended
  sources and normalizations. Both sieve inequalities still require proofs.
  The former harmonic hypothesis follows from Harmonique687.band_bound:
  for 2 <= z <= x, log(z) times the sum of 1/p over z < p <= x is at most
  log(x) - log(z) + 10. The proof uses the factorial convolution for the
  von Mangoldt function and Chebyshev's upper bound for its partial sums.

  For a qualitative saving, GainVariable687.exists_log_saving only needs
  some positive stock constant c and progression constant K, uniform over
  the cutoff exponents 0 < d <= 1/2. Each threshold in x may depend on d.
  Its proof chooses d = min(1/2, c/(6*K)), bounds the reciprocal-prime sum
  by 3*d*log(log x)/log x, and obtains exponent 2+2*d. The later theorem
  gain_from_stock supplies its progression hypothesis. The stronger fixed
  exponent 401/200 still has the numerical hypotheses of log_saving_of_two_sieves.

  For the survivor input, the authors' separate Lean 4.34.1 development
  contains source_root_survivor_lower with an existential positive constant
  in its own normalization. The passage to cutoff survivors is used in
  jacobsthal_of_source_root_lower. Neither declaration supplies the numerical
  coefficient 1/125 in this project's normalization as stated. A compatible
  replay and normalization of the published lower-sieve proof remain open here.
  https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/lean/OAI/NumberTheory/Jacobsthal/Conclusions/SourceRootCompletion.lean
  https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/lean/OAI/NumberTheory/Jacobsthal/Conclusions/JacobsthalFromRootLower.lean

  For the qualitative progression input, cutoffSurvivors_le_uniform_log
  in the same release bounds the number of survivors of [0,J) by
  255*(1+J/log J) whenever J <= z, with an arbitrary residue per prime
  through z. To retain the required dependence on the individual prime p,
  use Progressions687.charge_le_index_sieve with J = floor(N/p)+1.
  The upstream interval sieve has now been replayed in Lean 4.33.1. Its
  proof is due to OpenAI. The generator verifies each input's SHA-256,
  extracts the necessary finite-set definitions with their proofs, and
  replays both analytic modules. Two renamed Mathlib lemmas are substituted.
  The generator also accepts --sources /path/to/lean/OAI/NumberTheory/Jacobsthal
  for an offline rebuild from the pinned upstream checkout.
  CribleTransfert687.uniform_progression gives the absolute constant 1275
  for every 0 < d <= 1/2. It proves that J <= floor(z), log J >= log(x)/2,
  and 1 <= (N/p)/log(x) eventually, uniformly for primes floor(z)<p<=x.
  Thus gain_from_stock requires only the lower stock estimate for a
  uniform positive c. This does not supply the sharper 5/2 progression
  constant in the fixed exponent 401/200 argument.
  https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/lean/OAI/NumberTheory/Jacobsthal/Estimates/SquarefreeHarmonic.lean

  For the quadratic Iwaniec variant, H1 and H2 are hypotheses with the same
  absolute threshold, separating survivors at x/2 from the sum of the further
  deletions on [1,x²]. The old R1 split instead uses a shifted gap theorem and
  Mertens' product. The two splits are separate contributions.
  H2_from_progression discharges prime counting using normalized_prime_count.
  A uniform bound on the survivors in one large prime's progression still
  suffices for H2. H1 remains a separate sieve input.
  H2_from_index_sieve expresses that remaining upper estimate on an ordinary
  interval of floor(x^2/p)+1 consecutive indices with arbitrary residues.

  Separate route to the quadratic variant's H2 (pencil-and-paper argument,
  not checked in Lean here). Put z=floor(x/2). For p>z the index interval
  of length floor(x^2/p)+1 fits in five blocks of length z for large x.
  Translate each block's residues, extend the last block to length z, and
  apply the proved 255*(1+z/log z) sieve bound to each. All primes in
  (ceil(x/2),x] divide binom(x,floor(x/2)), whose size is at most 2^x;
  at most one endpoint prime was omitted. This gives asymptotic upper
  coefficient (5*255/2)*log 2 for total deletions in units x^2/(log x)^2.
  R2's threshold has coefficient 1000*(log 2)^2, strictly larger because
  log 2 > 0.6375. This avoids needing the smaller per-prime constant 32.

  The universal exclusion of 184 positions with primes through 71 and one
  extra singleton is certified computationally by two independent Python
  searches. The Lean witnesses at 183 and 189 and the implication forcing
  distance 146 between two still-uncovered points are separate results.

  The restricted one-singleton model, the cyclic gap formula, block estimates,
  semiprime loss construction in a thin or fixed-power band, grouped-capacity
  obstruction and common-representative obstruction are documented in the
  report and TeX note. The Lean sources give the finite or conditional pieces
  explicitly named there; the prose arguments retain their own proof status.
  Protection687.protect_band_product proves the finite survival-factor
  product underlying the simultaneous semiprime argument. The choice of
  semiprimes and the prime-number asymptotics in the TeX note are separate.
